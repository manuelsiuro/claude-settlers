# Sawmill Worker — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Sawmill Worker"** character in a low-poly 3D style.

The Sawmill Worker is a **resource processing serf** — working at the Sawmill to convert Wood (Logs) into Planks. This character should look practical and industrious, often seen carrying finished planks. A darker brown apron protects their clothing during the rough work of sawing.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the character. The final look should be industrious, practical, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. BASE MODEL & PROPORTIONS

Explain step-by-step how to model the Sawmill Worker character, starting from the shared base serf model:

- **The body:** An upright cylinder or slightly rounded cuboid (Light Brown), representing basic clothing. Keep proportions squat and chunky — approximately 1.5× as tall as wide for a charming low-poly feel.
- **The head:** A sphere (Light Peach) placed directly on top of the body. Scale it slightly larger than realistic for readability — about 60–70% of the body width.
- **The limbs (simplified):** Arms extend forward in a carrying pose, holding planks. Arms are small cylinders or thin cuboids (Light Brown).

## 3. PROFESSION-SPECIFIC FEATURES

Describe how to model the Sawmill Worker's distinguishing elements that set them apart from the base Transporter:

- **Carried planks (primary identifier):** 2–3 thin, flat cuboids (Tan) held horizontally between extended arms. The planks immediately communicate "I work with processed wood."
- **Dark brown apron (secondary identifier):** A flat cuboid (Dark Brown) attached to the front of the body, extending from chest to below the waist. This protects clothing during sawing work and provides visual contrast against the light brown body.
- **No hat or special tools:** The Sawmill Worker's identity comes from the carried planks and work apron — keep the design focused on these elements.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Use warm directional sunlight from above-left to clearly define the character's silhouette and the horizontal plank edges.
- A subtle ambient fill light to prevent harsh shadows on the small character.
- Present on a neutral ground plane — units are not shown with environmental context.
- The horizontal planks in the carrying pose should create a distinctive wide silhouette from the isometric game camera angle.
- The dark brown apron against the light brown body provides the secondary visual identification.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
