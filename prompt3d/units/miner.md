# Miner — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Miner"** character in a low-poly 3D style.

The Miner is a **resource gathering serf** — working deep inside mines to extract ores (Iron, Gold, Coal) and stone. Distinguished from the Stonemason by their protective helmet, the Miner works in dangerous underground conditions. The character should look rugged and protected, with a distinctive helmet and pickaxe.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the character. The final look should be tough, underground-ready, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Body / Clothing** — Light Brown (`#C4A56E`)

- **Helmet** — Yellow (`#F0C030`)
- **Pickaxe head** — Dark Grey (`#5A5A5A`)

## 2. BASE MODEL & PROPORTIONS

Explain step-by-step how to model the Miner character, starting from the shared base serf model:

- **The body:** An upright cylinder or slightly rounded cuboid (Light Brown), representing basic clothing. Keep proportions squat and chunky — approximately 1.5× as tall as wide for a charming low-poly feel.
- **The head:** A sphere (Light Peach) placed directly on top of the body. Scale it slightly larger than realistic for readability — about 60–70% of the body width.
- **The helmet:** A hemisphere or very short cylinder (Yellow) placed on top of the head sphere, covering the upper half. This is the Miner's most distinctive feature — visible from any angle.
- **The limbs (simplified):** One arm extends to the side holding the pickaxe. Arms are small cylinders or thin cuboids (Light Brown).

## 3. PROFESSION-SPECIFIC FEATURES

Describe how to model the Miner's distinguishing elements that set them apart from the base Transporter and from the Stonemason:

- **The helmet (primary identifier):** A hemisphere or very short cylinder (Yellow) sitting on top of the head. This is the key feature that differentiates the Miner from the Stonemason — both carry pickaxes, but only the Miner wears a helmet. The bright yellow color makes it visible even at small on-screen sizes.
- **The pickaxe (secondary identifier):** Same as the Stonemason's — a cuboid head (Dark Grey) with pointed ends on a thin cylinder handle (Tan). Attach it to one arm, held at the side or over the shoulder.
- **No apron or body modifications:** The Miner wears base clothing with just the helmet addition. The yellow helmet is sufficient to distinguish this profession.

---

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Use warm directional sunlight from above-left to clearly define the character's silhouette and make the yellow helmet glow.
- A subtle ambient fill light to prevent harsh shadows on the small character.
- Present on a neutral ground plane — units are not shown with environmental context.
- The bright yellow helmet should be the most visually striking element, immediately communicating the Miner's role from the isometric game camera angle.
- The helmet + pickaxe combination should read as distinctly different from the Stonemason (pickaxe only) even at a distance.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
