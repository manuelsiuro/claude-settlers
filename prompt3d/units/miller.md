# Miller — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Miller"** character in a low-poly 3D style.

The Miller is a **resource processing serf** — working at the Windmill to convert Grain into Flour. This character is perpetually covered in white flour dust, making them one of the most visually distinct serfs. The body color itself changes from the standard light brown to white/off-white, immediately setting the Miller apart.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the character. The final look should be dusty, flour-covered, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Body / Clothing (flour-dusted)** — White (`#F0F0F0`)
- **Head / Skin** — Light Peach (`#F5D5B8`)
- **Flour sack** — White (`#F0F0F0`)
- **Sack tie/detail** — Tan (`#D2A86E`)

---

## 2. BASE MODEL & PROPORTIONS

Explain step-by-step how to model the Miller character, starting from the shared base serf model with a key color change:

- **The body:** An upright cylinder or slightly rounded cuboid — but colored **White** (`#F0F0F0`) instead of the standard Light Brown. This flour-dusted body is the Miller's most immediate identifier. Keep proportions squat and chunky — approximately 1.5× as tall as wide.
- **The head:** A sphere (Light Peach) placed directly on top of the body. The head retains its skin tone — the flour coating is on the clothing, not the face.
- **The limbs (simplified):** Arms extend forward in a carrying pose, holding a flour sack. Arms are small cylinders or thin cuboids (White, matching the body).

---

## 3. PROFESSION-SPECIFIC FEATURES

Describe how to model the Miller's distinguishing elements that set them apart from the base Transporter:

- **Flour-dusted body (primary identifier):** The body is White (`#F0F0F0`) instead of the standard Light Brown. This single color change is the Miller's most powerful visual signal — they are the only serf with a white body, making them instantly recognizable even at a distance.
- **Flour sack (secondary identifier):** A rounded cuboid or sphere (White) held in the carrying pose between extended arms. Slightly smaller than the Transporter's generic parcel. Optionally add a tiny tied top — a small pinched cylinder or cone at the top of the sack, with a thin band (Tan) representing the tie.
- **No hat or special tools:** The Miller's identity comes entirely from the flour-white color scheme. Keep the design clean and let the color do the work.

---

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Use warm directional sunlight from above-left to clearly define the character's silhouette. The white body will be the brightest serf on screen.
- A subtle ambient fill light to add dimension to the white surfaces — pure white can look flat without careful lighting.
- Present on a neutral ground plane — units are not shown with environmental context.
- The white body should contrast strongly with the peach head and the ground, making the Miller immediately stand out from the isometric game camera angle.
- Consider a very slight warm tint to the white to prevent it from looking sterile — the flour should feel organic, not clinical.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
