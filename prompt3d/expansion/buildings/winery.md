# Winery — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Winery"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Winery is a **resource processing building** (Tier 3) — it employs a Winemaker who presses Grapes into Wine. Wine is a luxury good that boosts morale. The Winery should look like a solid stone building with an arched cellar entrance, surrounded by aged barrels and the rich aroma of fermentation — a refined, atmospheric scene.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be refined, aromatic, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Building walls** — Stone Grey (`#8A8A7A`)
- **Accent trim and grape motifs** — Deep Purple (`#5B1A6B`)
- **Barrels** — Barrel Brown (`#8B5E3C`)
- **Roof** — Dark Brown (`#5C3A1E`)
- **Wine liquid accents** — Wine Red (`#8B1A1A`)
- **Door, shutters, and beams** — Warm Wood (`#A0724A`)
- **Barrel bands and hardware** — Iron Band (`#4A4A4A`)

---

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Winery. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main building:** A medium-sized cuboid (Stone Grey walls) — slightly wider and taller than a typical processing building. Use a few thin cuboid trim pieces (Warm Wood) along the wall edges to suggest timber-reinforced stonework. The building conveys solidity and permanence.
- **The roof:** A steeply pitched two-plane roof (Dark Brown) with slightly overhanging eaves. Add thin cuboid rafters visible under the eave edges for structural detail.
- **The arched cellar entrance:** On the front face, model a prominent archway by placing a half-cylinder or beveled arch shape (Stone Grey, slightly darker) over a recessed rectangular doorway. Inside the recess, place a dark plane to suggest the cool cellar depths. This arch is the building's signature silhouette element.
- **The grape press:** Visible through a side opening or placed just outside the building — a short, wide cylinder base (Warm Wood) with a vertical screw mechanism (a thin cylinder topped with a horizontal cross-bar). This is the functional heart of the winery.
- **Stone steps:** 2–3 flat cuboid steps (Stone Grey) leading down from ground level to the cellar entrance, reinforcing the idea of an underground aging room.

---

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key winery elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **Wine barrels:** 2–3 barrels (Barrel Brown cylinders with slightly bulging profiles — scale the middle edge loop outward) placed outside near the cellar entrance. Add thin torus or flat cuboid rings (Iron Band) around each barrel for the metal hoops. Lay one barrel on its side on a small wooden cradle.
- **Grape crates:** 1–2 small open cuboid crates (Warm Wood) filled with tiny purple spheres (Deep Purple) — freshly delivered grapes awaiting pressing.
- **Wine bottles:** A cluster of 3–4 tiny elongated cylinders with tapered necks (Wine Red with a dark cap) on a small shelf or table near the entrance — finished product on display.
- **Grape press mechanism:** If not already part of the core structure, a wooden frame (Warm Wood cuboids) with a central screw (thin cylinder) and pressing plate — the key tool of the trade.
- **Stone steps to cellar:** Broad, flat cuboid steps (Stone Grey) descending into the arched entrance, with subtle wear suggested by slightly rounded top edges (bevel the top face).
- **A wooden sign:** A small hanging sign (Warm Wood flat cuboid on two tiny chain links) with a grape cluster silhouette — identifying the building at a glance.
- **Spilled wine stain:** A tiny flat irregular shape (Wine Red, very low opacity or darkened) on the stone steps near the barrels — a charming detail suggesting busy production.
- **Vine decoration:** A small cluster of vine leaves (a few flat diamond shapes in a muted green) growing up one side of the archway, tying the building visually to its vineyard source.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Cool, even directional light that plays off the Stone Grey walls, giving the building a dignified, solid presence.
- A warm secondary light angled into the cellar archway, creating a gentle glow from within that suggests the warmth of fermentation.
- The Deep Purple and Wine Red accents should stand out against the neutral stone — they are the building's identity colors.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the arched cellar entrance, outdoor barrels, and grape press should all be visible, composing a scene of artisanal production.
- The overall atmosphere should feel refined and established — this is not a rough workshop but a place where raw ingredients are transformed into something valuable.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
