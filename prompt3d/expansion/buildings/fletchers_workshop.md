# Fletcher's Workshop — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Fletcher's Workshop"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Fletcher's Workshop is a **resource processing building** (Tier 3) — it employs a Fletcher who crafts Arrows from Wood and Iron Bars, supplying ammunition for archers and ranged military units. The building should look like a precise, orderly workshop with bundles of arrows, a workbench, and an archery target for testing — a place where patience and accuracy produce deadly projectiles.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be precise, military-adjacent, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Fletcher's Workshop. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main workshop:** A medium-sized cuboid building (Wood Brown walls) with the front wall partially open — use a half-wall on the lower portion and leave the upper half open to reveal the workbench inside. This open-front design lets the Fletcher work with natural light and allows the player to see the crafting activity. Add thin cuboid beam supports where the upper wall is removed.
- **The pitched roof:** A standard gable roof (Dark Brown) with a moderate slope. Extend the eaves well forward over the open front, creating a generous overhang that shelters the workbench from weather. Add visible rafter ends along the eaves — thin cuboid shapes poking out from beneath the roof edge.
- **The side walls:** Full-height solid cuboid walls (Wood Brown) on both sides and the back. These enclose the workshop and contrast with the open front. Add 1-2 small rectangular window openings on one side wall for cross-ventilation.
- **The foundation:** A low, flat cuboid platform (Dark Brown) extending slightly beyond the walls — a simple wooden floor raised off the ground.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key fletcher's workshop elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **Arrow bundles:** The primary visual product. Create groups of 6-8 very thin, long cylinders (Arrow Shaft Tan) bundled together and bound with a tiny ring (Dark Brown). Tip each shaft with a tiny diamond or pyramid shape (Iron Grey) for the arrowhead, and add tiny flat planes (Fletching Green) at the tail end for feather fletchings. Place 3-4 bundles around the workshop — some on the workbench, some standing in barrel quivers, some stacked against the wall.
- **Archery target:** A prominent disc shape (Target White base) on a thin cuboid easel stand. Paint concentric rings using smaller discs layered on the front face — outer ring (Target Red), middle (Target White), bullseye (Target Red). Stick 2-3 arrow shafts into the target face at angles. Place the target a short distance from the workshop — the Fletcher tests their arrows.
- **Workbench:** A solid cuboid table (Wood Brown) positioned inside the workshop, visible through the open front. Place small props on it: a knife (thin flat blade shape, Iron Grey), loose feathers (tiny flat planes, Fletching Green), and arrow shafts in progress.
- **Bow rack:** A vertical wooden frame (Dark Brown) with 2-3 curved thin shapes resting in notches — bows ready for stringing. Place this against one interior wall. The Fletcher may also repair bows.
- **Iron bar stack (input):** 3-4 thin, flat, elongated cuboids (Iron Grey) stacked neatly near the workshop entrance — raw material for forging arrowheads.
- **Wood shavings:** Small, thin, curled flat shapes (Arrow Shaft Tan, lighter) scattered on the ground near the workbench — evidence of shaft whittling. Use tiny flat planes at random angles.
- **Feather basket:** A small woven basket (Dark Brown tapered cylinder) filled with tiny flat planes in various greens and white — feathers collected for fletching.
- **Quiver barrel:** A tall, narrow cylinder (Dark Brown) standing upright near the output side, filled with finished arrows standing point-up — a storage and transport container.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Clean, bright directional sunlight — the Fletcher's Workshop is a place of precision, and the light should be clear and even to show the fine details of the arrows and target.
- The red-and-white archery target should be the brightest, most eye-catching element — angle the key light to fully illuminate its face.
- A warm fill light inside the workshop, visible through the open front, illuminating the workbench and the arrows in progress.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the open front of the workshop and the archery target should both be visible, with arrow bundles scattered between them.
- The ground should be clean packed earth or light grass — this is a tidy, organized workplace.
- The overall atmosphere should feel sharp, methodical, and purposeful — every arrow is crafted with care, and the target proves each one flies true.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
