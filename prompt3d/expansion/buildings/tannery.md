# Tannery — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Tannery"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Tannery is a **resource processing building** (Tier 3) — it employs a Tanner who converts raw Leather into worked Leather, a key material for armor and equipment. The building should look like a rugged, industrial workshop with stretched animal hides on drying racks and large soaking vats — a place of hard, messy labor with an earthy, leather-brown palette.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be industrial, rugged, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Tannery. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main workshop:** A medium-sized cuboid building (Warm Brown walls) with one entire front wall removed to expose the workspace interior. The open front conveys the need for airflow — tanning is a pungent process. Use thin cuboid beams along the opening's edges to frame it structurally.
- **The roof:** A low-pitched gable roof made from two angled planes (Dark Brown). Extend the roof eaves slightly beyond the walls, especially over the open front, to create a sheltered overhang that protects the hides from rain. Add thin cuboid rafters visible from the open side.
- **The stone foundation:** A flat, slightly wider cuboid base (Stone Grey) beneath the entire building. Extend it a half-unit beyond the walls on all sides to give the structure a solid, grounded feeling. Use a slightly rough look — offset a few small cubes along the edges.
- **The chimney:** A short, square cuboid chimney (Stone Grey) rising from the rear of the roof. The tanning process involves heated vats, so a small chimney is necessary. Keep it modest — not as tall as a smelter's, but clearly functional.
- **The interior floor:** A flat plane (Dark Brown, slightly darker than the walls) visible through the open front. This gives depth to the workspace and provides a surface for interior props.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key tannery elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **Stretched hides on racks:** The signature visual element. Build 2-3 wooden drying racks from thin cuboid posts (Dark Grey) forming an A-frame or H-frame structure. Stretch flat, thin cuboid planes (Leather Tan) across each rack — these represent animal hides pulled taut to dry. Vary the sizes slightly and angle the racks so they catch the light differently. Place them beside and in front of the workshop.
- **Tanning vats:** 2 large open-topped cylinders (Dark Grey exterior, Water Brown liquid inside) placed within or just outside the workshop. Model the liquid as a flat disc inside each cylinder, slightly below the rim. These are the soaking vats where raw hides are processed with tannins.
- **Raw hide pile (input):** A small mound of irregularly stacked flat cuboids (Leather Tan, slightly lighter) near the workshop entrance — untreated hides awaiting processing. Stack them loosely, with edges overlapping.
- **Finished leather stack (output):** Neatly folded flat cuboids (Hide Beige) stacked in a tidy pile on the opposite side of the entrance — the worked leather ready for transport. These should look more uniform and carefully arranged than the raw pile.
- **Scraping tools:** 2-3 thin, flat blade shapes (Dark Grey) leaning against the wall or resting on a small workbench cuboid — the tools used to scrape fat and hair from hides.
- **Drying line:** A thin cylinder rope (Dark Brown) strung between two small posts near the building, with 2-3 small flat rectangles (Hide Beige) draped over it — additional hides air-drying in the breeze.
- **Water bucket:** A small tapered cylinder (Dark Brown with a thin cuboid handle) near the vats — used for carrying water to and from the vats.
- **Bark pile:** A small mound of tiny brown cubes (Warm Brown) near the vats — tree bark is a traditional source of tannin.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- A warm, slightly muted directional light — the Tannery is an earthy, working-class building, not a bright, cheerful one. Golden-brown tones should dominate.
- A subtle warm fill light inside the workshop, casting through the open front wall to illuminate the interior props and vats.
- The stretched hides on the racks are the focal point — angle the key light so the flat hide surfaces catch broad highlights against their darker wooden frames.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the open front of the workshop, the drying racks, and the vats should all be clearly visible.
- The ground around the Tannery should feel worn and muddy — use a slightly darker earth tone for the surrounding base.
- The overall atmosphere should feel industrious and earthy — a hardworking place where raw materials are transformed through skilled labor.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
