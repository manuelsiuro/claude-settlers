# Weaver's Hut — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Weaver's Hut"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Weaver's Hut is a **resource processing building** (Tier 3) — it employs a Weaver who transforms raw Wool into Cloth, a material used for uniforms and trade goods. The building should look like a cozy, small domestic workshop with a loom visible through a wide window and colorful cloth draped over the sill — a warm, inviting cottage radiating craft and color.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be domestic, colorful, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Weaver's Hut. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main hut:** A small, compact cuboid building (Warm Brown walls) — this is a cozy cottage, not a large workshop. Keep the footprint modest, about 70% the size of a standard processing building. The warmth comes from the proportions — slightly wider than it is deep, giving a squat, inviting shape.
- **The wide window:** A large rectangular opening cut into the front wall, framed by thin cuboids (Light Brown). This is the defining architectural feature — the window is oversized to let light fall on the loom inside, and it serves as a display for the Weaver's work. Model a low sill (thin flat cuboid) at the bottom of the opening.
- **The roof:** A pitched gable roof (Dark Brown) with a moderate slope. Add thin cuboid fascia boards along the eaves. The roofline should feel homey — not too steep, not too flat. Consider a slight overhang above the front window to create a charming awning effect.
- **The door:** A small, simple rectangular door (Dark Brown, slightly darker than the roof) on one side wall. Add a tiny cuboid handle. The door is secondary to the window as a visual element.
- **The interior floor:** A visible flat plane through the window opening (Light Brown) that grounds the interior props. This gives the scene depth when viewed from the isometric angle.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key weaver's hut elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **The loom (visible through window):** The centerpiece prop. Build a rectangular wooden frame (Warm Brown) from thin cuboid beams — two vertical posts connected by a top crossbar and a bottom rail. String thin cylinders or flat planes between the posts to represent the warp threads (alternate Cream Cloth and Blue Thread colors for visual interest). Add a small crossbar shuttle resting in the middle of the threads. The loom should be positioned just behind the window so it is clearly visible from outside.
- **Draped cloth on windowsill:** A flat, slightly curved plane (Cream Cloth) draped over the window sill, hanging partway down the exterior wall. This is a key storytelling element — the Weaver displays finished work. Add a subtle fold by using 2-3 connected angled planes rather than one flat piece.
- **Wool baskets:** 2 woven baskets (Light Brown tapered cylinders) placed near the hut entrance, overflowing with puffy wool. Model the wool as small clustered icospheres (Wool White) poking above the basket rims — soft, fluffy, and inviting.
- **Thread spools:** 4-5 small cylinders (alternating Blue Thread, Red Thread, Cream Cloth) arranged on a small shelf or scattered near the window. These add vibrant pops of color to the scene — the Weaver's raw materials in their most colorful form.
- **Finished cloth bolts:** 2-3 tightly rolled cylinders (Cream Cloth, with one in Blue Thread) stacked neatly near the output side of the hut. These are the finished product — uniform, smooth rolls ready for delivery.
- **A spinning wheel (optional):** A small circle (Light Brown) made from a torus shape with thin cuboid spokes and a stand — placed beside the hut. This secondary tool adds visual richness and tells the full story of wool-to-cloth.
- **Flower box:** A thin cuboid planter (Dark Brown) mounted below the window, filled with tiny colorful sphere flowers (Red Thread, Blue Thread) — the Weaver takes pride in their home.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Bright, warm sunlight — the Weaver's Hut is a cheerful, domestic scene. Use a golden directional light that makes the cream cloth glow warmly.
- A soft interior fill light visible through the wide window, illuminating the loom and making the thread colors visible even from outside.
- The colorful thread spools and draped cloth should pop against the brown wood — these small accents of blue, red, and cream are the visual highlights.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the wide window with the loom inside should face the camera, with the draped cloth and wool baskets clearly visible.
- The surrounding ground should be clean and grassy (light green) — this is a tidy, well-kept cottage, not an industrial site.
- The overall atmosphere should feel warm, creative, and homey — a place where raw fleece becomes beautiful fabric through patient skill.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
