# Well — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Well"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Well is a **resource gathering building** (Tier 1) — it requires no worker and automatically produces Water Barrels. Water is a fundamental resource needed by the Brewery and other production chains. The Well should look like a classic medieval stone well with a rope-and-bucket mechanism and a small peaked roof — a simple, essential village structure.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be simple, essential, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Well wall stones** — Stone Grey (`#8A8A7A`)
- **Water surface and accents** — Water Blue (`#5599CC`)
- **Rope** — Rope Tan (`#D2A86E`)
- **Cross-beam and roof frame** — Wood Brown (`#A0724A`)
- **Lower stones and base** — Dark Stone (`#5A5A5A`)
- **Bucket** — Bucket Grey (`#7A7A7A`)

---

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Well. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The well wall:** A short, open-topped cylinder (Stone Grey) — this is the circular stone well rim. Use a cylinder with 8–12 sides to keep it low-poly while still reading as round. Scale it to be about waist-height. Add a ring of slightly darker cuboid stones (Dark Stone) around the base to suggest a reinforced foundation. Optionally, use a slightly wider bottom and narrower top (scale the top face inward slightly) for a tapered, old-world feel.
- **The cross-beam and supports:** Two vertical cuboid posts (Wood Brown) rising from opposite sides of the well rim, connected at the top by a horizontal cylindrical cross-beam. These posts should extend well above the well, roughly 2x the height of the stone wall. Anchor them into or just outside the stone rim.
- **The peaked roof:** A small two-plane pitched roof (Wood Brown) sitting atop the cross-beam, just wide enough to shelter the well opening from rain. Use two flat cuboid planes meeting at a ridge — a miniature gable roof. This roof is the element that makes the well recognizable from a distance.
- **The rope and bucket mechanism:** A thin cylinder rope (Rope Tan) wound around the horizontal cross-beam, dangling down into the well. At the end of the rope, attach a small tapered cylinder bucket (Bucket Grey). Model the winch as a small cylinder drum on the cross-beam with a cuboid handle crank extending to one side.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key well elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **The water bucket:** A small tapered cylinder (Bucket Grey, wider at the top, narrower at the bottom) with a thin half-torus wire handle. Position it either hanging from the rope mid-way down, or resting on the well rim as if just pulled up. Add a tiny blue disc (Water Blue) inside the bucket to show it is full of water.
- **The water barrel (output):** A short, wide cylinder (Wood Brown with dark bands) placed on the ground beside the well — the filled output barrel ready for a Transporter to collect. Add 2 thin torus rings (Dark Stone) as metal barrel bands.
- **The stone path:** 3–5 flat, slightly irregular cuboids (Stone Grey, Dark Stone) arranged in a short path leading away from the well — suggesting foot traffic from villagers coming to draw water.
- **The puddle:** A small, flat disc or irregular shape (Water Blue, very thin) on the ground near the base of the well — water splashed while drawing buckets. This tiny detail adds life and believability.
- **The wooden winch handle:** A small cuboid crank arm (Wood Brown) extending from one end of the horizontal cross-beam drum — the mechanism for raising and lowering the bucket.
- **Moss or ivy detail:** A few tiny flat shapes (a muted green, slightly darker than grass) clinging to the lower stones of the well wall — the well has been here a long time and moisture encourages growth.
- **A second bucket:** A small bucket (Bucket Grey) sitting upside-down on the ground near the well — a spare, adding visual interest and asymmetry.
- **Ground vegetation:** A ring of small grass cone shapes around the well base, slightly lusher and greener near the puddle — water encourages growth.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Clean, neutral directional sunlight — the Well is a simple structure, so the lighting should be straightforward and clear. The Stone Grey should read as solid and dependable.
- A subtle cool blue ambient bounce near the well opening and puddle, suggesting reflected light from the water below.
- The Water Blue accents (puddle, bucket water, barrel) should provide the key color contrast against the grey stone — small but vivid touches of blue that tell the story.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the peaked roof, hanging bucket, and output water barrel should all be visible. The well's compact vertical form (posts, roof, rope) should read clearly as a distinct silhouette.
- The overall atmosphere should feel village-essential and timeless — a structure so fundamental that every settlement needs one, quietly providing the most basic resource of all.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
