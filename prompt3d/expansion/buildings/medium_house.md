# Medium House — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Medium House"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Medium House is a **housing building** (Tier 2) — a comfortable two-story half-timber dwelling that shelters up to 16 settlers. It represents the growing prosperity of the settlement, a step up from the humble cottage. The Medium House should look established and well-built — visible dark timber framing against pale plaster walls, a steep pitched roof, and an overhanging second floor in the classic medieval half-timber style.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be comfortable, established, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Medium House. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The ground floor:** A cuboid base (Beige Plaster) sitting on a Stone Grey foundation strip. The ground floor is the structural base and should feel solid. Apply the half-timber pattern by placing thin flat cuboid strips (Dark Brown Timber) along the edges, corners, and in diagonal cross-bracing patterns on each visible face. The contrast between light plaster and dark timber is the building's defining visual signature.
- **The overhanging second floor:** A slightly wider cuboid (Beige Plaster with Dark Brown Timber framing) placed on top of the ground floor, extending 10–15% beyond the lower walls on the front and sides. This jettied overhang is a hallmark of medieval half-timber construction. Support the overhang with 2–3 small bracket cuboids (Dark Brown Timber) beneath the extended floor edge.
- **The steep pitched roof:** A tall, steeply angled two-plane roof (Dark Brown Roof) with the ridge running along the building's length. The steep pitch gives the house its vertical, prosperous silhouette. Extend the eaves well beyond the walls for visual weight.
- **The windows:** 2 windows on the ground floor and 2–3 on the second floor, each a small recessed square with a cross-mullion (Dark Brown Timber) and a glowing plane behind (Window Glow). The second-floor windows should be slightly larger or more numerous, reflecting the more comfortable living quarters above.
- **The front door:** A sturdy rectangular cuboid (Warm Brown) set into the ground floor center, with a small arched top (a half-cylinder or beveled top edge). A tiny cylinder doorknob and a small flat cuboid step in front.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key medium house elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

The chimney should be proportionally larger than the Small House chimney — this is a bigger home with a proper hearth. Cap the top with a slightly wider flat cuboid to prevent rain ingress.
- **Window shutters:** Pairs of small thin cuboid shutters (Warm Brown) flanking each window, mounted flat against the wall in the open position. On one window, angle a shutter slightly outward (rotated 20 degrees) for visual variety and life.
- **Second-floor balcony railing:** A small flat cuboid platform (Dark Brown Timber) extending from one second-floor window, with a thin cuboid railing made of vertical posts and a horizontal top rail. Just large enough to lean out — a charming detail suggesting domestic life above the street.
