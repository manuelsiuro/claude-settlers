# Siege Workshop — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Siege Workshop"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Siege Workshop is a **resource processing building** (Tier 3) — it employs an Engineer who constructs massive Siege Rams from Wood and Iron Bars. This is the largest and heaviest processing building in the game, producing the ultimate tools of war. The building should look like a sprawling, open-sided industrial hall dominated by heavy timber framing and the imposing silhouette of a battering ram under construction.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be heavy, imposing, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Siege Workshop. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main hall:** The largest processing building footprint in the game. Model it as a wide, tall, open-sided structure — 4-6 heavy timber posts (Dark Wood thick cuboids) supporting a massive roof, with no full walls between the posts. The open sides allow the movement of enormous siege equipment in and out. Think of a medieval timber-frame barn scaled up for war machines.
- **The heavy roof:** A broad, high-pitched gable roof (Dark Wood) spanning the entire structure. Use two large angled planes with visible heavy rafter beams underneath (Warm Brown cuboids running from ridge to eaves). The roof peak should be the tallest point — high enough to accommodate the battering ram on its cradle beneath.
- **The stone foundation:** A thick, wide cuboid platform (Stone Grey) forming the workshop floor. This needs to be visually solid — heavy siege equipment requires a stable base. Extend the foundation slightly beyond the post line on all sides.
- **The timber posts:** 4-6 thick square cuboid columns (Dark Wood) — these are the structural backbone. Make them visibly beefy compared to other buildings' structural elements. Add diagonal brace cuboids between the posts and the roof beams for a convincing timber-frame look.
- **The rear wall:** Unlike the open sides, the back of the workshop has a full wall (Warm Brown) — a solid backdrop for storing materials and tools. This single enclosed wall grounds the structure visually.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key siege workshop elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **The battering ram:** The centerpiece prop and the building's entire reason for existence. Model a massive, long cylinder (Dark Wood) — the main ram log — suspended horizontally on a wooden cradle frame (Warm Brown cuboid supports). Cap the front end with a large, faceted cone or blunted pyramid shape (Iron Grey) — the iron head designed to smash gates. The ram should be clearly the largest single prop in the scene, stretching nearly the full length of the workshop.
- **Iron bar stacks (input):** Multiple piles of thin, flat cuboids (Iron Grey) stacked in orderly groups near the rear wall — the raw iron waiting to be forged into ram heads and fittings. Use more bars than in smaller workshops to convey the massive material cost.
- **Heavy timber pile (input):** Large, thick cylinders (Warm Brown) stacked in a broad pyramid near one open side — entire tree trunks, not planks. These are much larger than a normal woodpile, scaled to match the size of siege equipment.
- **Chains:** Loops of chain modeled as linked torus shapes or a draped series of small connected rings (Chain Grey) hanging from the roof beams or coiled on the floor. Chains are used to hoist and suspend the heavy ram during construction.
- **Forge area:** A small anvil (Iron Grey cuboid on a Wood stump) and a contained fire pit (small cuboid with an Ember-colored interior) in one corner — the Engineer heats and shapes the iron fittings here. Smaller and more focused than a full Blacksmith's forge.
- **Workbench with blueprints:** A large, sturdy cuboid table (Warm Brown) with a flat white rectangle on top representing plans and schematics. Add small tool shapes — hammers, chisels — as tiny cuboid props on the bench surface.
- **Rope coils:** 2-3 torus shapes (Rope Tan) laid flat on the ground or hanging from pegs on the rear wall — heavy rope for lashing, hauling, and rigging the ram's suspension system.
- **Wooden wheels (optional):** 1-2 large disc shapes (Warm Brown) with cuboid spoke patterns leaning against a post — wheels for the ram's mobile carriage.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Strong, dramatic directional light entering through the open sides of the workshop, casting deep shadows from the heavy timber posts across the stone floor and the battering ram.
- The iron ram head should catch a specular-like highlight — make it the hardest, most reflective-looking surface in the scene against the warm wood tones.
- A warm secondary fill from the small forge area, casting a subtle orange pool of light in one corner.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the open sides should reveal the battering ram as the unmistakable focal point. Position the camera so the full length of the ram is visible.
- The ground around the Siege Workshop should be churned earth (Earth Brown) — heavy equipment and foot traffic have torn up the terrain.
- The overall atmosphere should feel powerful, industrial, and warlike — this is where the mightiest weapon on the battlefield is born, one iron fitting and timber joint at a time.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
