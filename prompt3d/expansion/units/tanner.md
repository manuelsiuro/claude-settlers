# Tanner — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Tanner"** character in a low-poly 3D style.

The Tanner is a **resource processing serf** — working at the Tannery to scrape and treat raw hides into worked Leather. This character does heavy, messy physical labor and should look rugged and industrial, with a thick dark brown leather apron, dark protective gloves, and a scraping knife.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the character. The final look should be rugged, industrial, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

## 2. BASE MODEL & PROPORTIONS

Explain step-by-step how to model the Tanner character, starting from the shared base serf model:

- **The body:** An upright cylinder or slightly rounded cuboid (Light Brown), representing basic clothing. Keep proportions squat and chunky — approximately 1.5x as tall as wide for a charming low-poly feel.
- **The head:** A sphere (Light Peach) placed directly on top of the body. Scale it slightly larger than realistic for readability — about 60–70% of the body width.
- **No hat:** The Tanner goes bareheaded — the dark apron and gloves provide all the visual identity needed. The bare head keeps the silhouette focused on the heavy apron below.
- **The limbs (simplified):** One arm extends forward holding the scraping knife. Both hands are covered by dark gloves. Arms are small cylinders or thin cuboids (Light Brown), with the hand portions swapped to the darker glove color.

## 3. PROFESSION-SPECIFIC FEATURES

Describe how to model the Tanner's distinguishing elements that set them apart from the base Transporter:

- **The dark brown leather apron (primary identifier):** A flat cuboid (Dark Brown `#5C3A1E`) attached to the front of the body, extending from chest to below the knees — slightly longer than most other aprons. Make this apron noticeably thicker (deeper in the Z-axis) than the flat cloth aprons worn by the Baker or Butcher, suggesting heavy, stiff leather rather than fabric. The dark brown is the darkest apron color among all serfs, immediately reading as tough, protective workwear. The color contrast against the lighter body clothing is dramatic.
- **The dark gloves (co-primary identifier):** Small cuboids or short cylinders (Dark `#4A3520`) replacing the default hand shapes at the ends of both arms. The dark gloves are a unique feature — no other serf wears them. They signal that the Tanner handles harsh chemicals and rough materials. The very dark color at the extremities of the arms draws the eye and adds to the rugged, industrial feel.
- **The scraping knife (secondary identifier):** A flat, wide rectangular cuboid blade (Grey `#7A7A7A`) held in one gloved hand. The blade should be wider and shorter than a sword or cleaver — more like a broad, flat spatula shape used to scrape hides. Keep it about 60% of the arm's length. The flat grey blade catches the light and provides a metallic accent against the dark brown palette.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Use warm but slightly muted directional sunlight from above-left — the Tanner is an indoor workshop character, so avoid overly bright, cheerful lighting.
- A subtle neutral ambient fill to keep the dark brown apron and dark gloves readable without losing their heavy, shadowed quality.
- Present on a neutral ground plane — units are not shown with environmental context.
- The dark brown apron should be the most prominent feature from the isometric game camera angle — its deep, dark tone creates the strongest contrast against the light brown body of any apron-wearing serf.
- The dark gloves at the arm tips and the grey scraping knife should provide secondary visual interest, reinforcing the "heavy labor" character.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
