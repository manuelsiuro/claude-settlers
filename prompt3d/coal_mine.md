# Coal Mine — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Coal Mine"** in a low-poly 3D style using software like Blender or Maya.

The Coal Mine is a **resource gathering building** — it employs a Miner who extracts Coal Ore from mountain deposits. Coal is a critical fuel used by the Iron Smelter, Blacksmith, Goldsmith, and Bakery. The structure shares the general mine layout but is identified by a distinctive black ore indicator.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be dark, sooty, industrial, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Mine entrance structure** — Dark Grey (`#5A5A5A`)
- **Mine shaft opening** — Very Dark / Black (`#1A1A1A`)
- **Coal ore indicator** — Black (`#2A2A2A`)
- **Mountain rock** — Rocky Grey (`#8A8A8A`) and Dark Rock (`#6A6A6A`)
- **Support timber beams** — Dark Brown (`#5C3A1E`)
- **Coal chunks** — Coal Black (`#1E1E1E`) with slight Charcoal sheen (`#3A3A3A`)
- **Ground/dirt** — Darkened Earth (`#5A4A3A`) — stained by coal dust
- **Rail track** — Dark Steel (`#4A4A4A`)
- **Ore cart** — Weathered Grey (`#6A6A6A`)

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Coal Mine. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The mountain backdrop:** A large angular mass of cuboids (Rocky Grey, Dark Rock) suggesting the mountain face. Darker than the Iron Mine's mountain — coal dust stains everything.
- **The mine entrance structure:** A dark grey cuboid (Dark Grey) built flush against the mountain — the reinforced entrance frame. Same general proportions as other mines.
- **The mine shaft opening:** A smaller, very dark rectangle on the front face — the tunnel entrance. Coal mines feel particularly dark.
- **Support timber beams:** Thin cuboids (Dark Brown) framing the shaft — these should look slightly more weathered/darker than the Iron Mine's timbers, stained by coal dust.
- **The coal ore indicator:** A small black sphere or cube (Black `#2A2A2A`) placed near the entrance. The black indicator distinguishes this from Iron (red), Gold (yellow), and Stone (light grey) mines.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key coal mine elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **Coal pile:** A mound of small black cubes and spheres (Coal Black) near the entrance — darker and more irregular than iron ore. This is the building's output.
- **Ore cart:** A small boxy shape (Weathered Grey) filled with black coal chunks, sitting on rail tracks.
- **Rail tracks:** Two thin parallel cuboids extending from the entrance.
- **Soot and dust:** The ground around the mine should be darker than usual — Dark Brown/Charcoal tones radiating outward from the entrance, suggesting coal dust contamination.
- **A lantern:** Especially important here — a small cuboid with warm yellow glow near the entrance. Coal mines are extremely dark.
- **Pickaxe and shovel:** Small tool props leaning against the entrance.
- **Stacked timber:** Horizontal brown cylinders for tunnel support beams.
- **A ventilation shaft (optional):** A small vertical cylinder poking up from the mountain above the entrance — coal mines need ventilation.
- **Sparse, darkened vegetation:** Any nearby plants should look dulled and darkened by coal dust.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Strong directional light to create contrast against the predominantly dark palette.
- Cool, slightly bluish ambient fill to keep the dark elements readable without muddying them.
- The overall scene should feel noticeably darker than other mines — coal is a grimy business.
- The black ore indicator should be distinguishable from the shaft darkness through slightly different positioning and a hint of charcoal sheen.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the coal pile and dark entrance should be the focal points.
- Despite the dark palette, shapes should still read clearly — use subtle value contrasts between coal chunks, ground, and structure.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
