# Toolmaker's Workshop — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Toolmaker's Workshop"** in a low-poly 3D style.

The Toolmaker's Workshop is a **resource processing building** — it crafts Tools (Axes, Pickaxes, Scythes, Hammers, Fishing Rods) from Iron Bars and Planks. Tools are essential for many worker professions. The building should look like a skilled craftsman's workshop, with an anvil and tools on display.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be crafty, workshop-like, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

## 1. THE COLOR PALETTE

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Main building walls** — Brown (`#8B5E3C`)
- **Roof** — Grey (`#808080`)
- **Anvil** — Dark Steel Grey (`#5A5A5A`)
- **Iron bars (input)** — Silver-Grey (`#A0A0A0`)
- **Planks (input)** — Pale Yellow-Brown (`#DCC8A0`)
- **Tools (output)** — Steel Grey (`#7A7A7A`) with Brown handles (`#A0724A`)
- **Door and window frames** — Dark Brown (`#5C3A1E`)
- **Workbench** — Warm Brown (`#A0724A`)
- **Foundation** — Earthy Brown (`#6B4226`)

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the Toolmaker's Workshop. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The main building:** A medium cuboid (Brown). Standard workshop size — bigger than a hut, but not as large as the Smelter or Barracks.
- **The roof:** A sloped cuboid roof (Grey). The grey roof distinguishes the Toolmaker from other brown-walled buildings — it suggests a more industrial, metalworking character.
- **The door:** A standard rectangular doorway on the front face.
- **Windows:** 1–2 windows, one with shutters partially open (thin cuboid shapes).
- **A workshop extension:** A small open-sided lean-to or awning on one side (Brown posts, Grey flat roof) — the outdoor workspace where the anvil sits.

---

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key toolmaker elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **The anvil:** The signature prop — a small T-shaped structure made of two grey cuboids (Dark Steel Grey). A wider flat top on a narrower pedestal. Place it prominently under the workshop extension, in clear view.
- **Iron bars (input):** 2–3 thin, elongated cuboids (Silver-Grey) leaning against the workshop wall or stacked on a rack.
- **Planks (input):** A few thin flat cuboids (Pale Yellow-Brown) stacked nearby — the other input material.
- **Finished tools (output):** The most fun props — model 2–3 miniature tool shapes:
  - **Axe:** Small wedge head (Steel Grey) on a thin cuboid handle (Brown).
  - **Pickaxe:** Two-pointed head on a handle.
  - **Hammer:** Rectangular head on a handle.
  Place these on a display rack (thin horizontal cuboid on posts) near the entrance, showing the building's diverse output.
- **A workbench:** A flat cuboid table (Warm Brown) inside or near the workshop extension with small tool shapes and metal scraps on it.
- **A grindstone:** A thin cylinder (grey) on a small frame — for sharpening tools.
- **Metal scraps:** Tiny scattered silver-grey cubes around the anvil area.
- **A bucket of water:** Small cylinder near the anvil — for quenching hot metal.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Warm, clear directional sunlight illuminating the workshop extension and anvil.
- The contrast between the brown walls and grey roof should be clear — two distinct materials.
- Good lighting on the tool display rack — the variety of tools is the visual storytelling element.
- A subtle warm ambient fill to keep the workshop feeling inviting and productive.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the anvil and tool display should be prominently visible, facing the camera.
- The overall feel should be skilled and industrious — a master craftsman's domain where raw materials become essential tools.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
