# Woodcutter's Hut — 3D Modeling Prompt

Act as an expert 3D artist and instructor specializing in low-poly modeling and stylized game art. Your task is to provide a comprehensive, step-by-step guide on how to model a stylized **"Woodcutter's Hut"** in a low-poly 3D style using software like Blender or Maya.

The Woodcutter's Hut is a **resource gathering building** — it employs a Woodcutter who chops down nearby trees to produce Wood (Logs). It should look cozy, rustic, and small — a humble forest worker's dwelling surrounded by evidence of its trade.

Please provide a structured, detailed explanation covering the geometry, specific shapes, and color palettes for each part of the scene. The final look should be cozy, rustic, and characteristic of a classic fantasy low-poly aesthetic (flat shading, distinct geometric shapes, no complex textures).

---

Provide a specific low-poly color palette with exact HEX codes for:

- **Main wood walls** — Brown (`#8B5E3C`)

- **Log pile / raw wood** — Light Brown (`#C4A56E`)
- **Foundation/ground** — Earthy Brown (`#6B4226`)

- **Grass tufts** — Forest Green (`#4A7A3D`)

## 2. CORE STRUCTURE & GEOMETRY

Explain step-by-step how to model the main hut. Include descriptions of the shapes and primitive meshes to use for:

- **The foundation:** A very low, slightly larger-than-the-hut cuboid to ground it (Earthy Brown). This is a small-footprint building.
- **The walls:** A small, low cuboid (Brown). The hut is humble — keep proportions squat and cozy. Slightly exaggerate the width relative to height for a chunky low-poly feel.
- **The roof:** A slightly larger, shallow-angled cuboid placed on top of the walls, creating overhanging eaves on all sides (Dark Brown). The roof should extend past the walls to give a sheltered look. Alternatively, a simple two-plane pitched roof.
- **The door:** A darker rectangular indentation on one face of the hut walls.
- **A window:** A small square indentation on the side wall, optionally with a tiny cross-beam divider.

## 3. PROPS AND ENVIRONMENT DETAILS

Describe how to model the key woodcutter elements to tell a story. Break down the modeling steps for:

- **The chopping block (tree stump):** A short, wide cylinder (Light Tan) placed beside the hut — the iconic work surface.
- **The woodcutter's axe:** A thin cuboid handle (Tan) with a small wedge or flat cuboid axe head (Steel Grey) embedded in the stump or leaning against it.
- **Log pile:** A small stack of 2–3 short horizontal cylinders (Light Brown) beside the hut, representing the building's output goods.
- **Whole tree trunk:** One longer cylinder on the ground nearby, as if recently felled and awaiting splitting.
- **Surrounding low-poly flora:** A few small cone-shaped pine trees (Forest Green), grass tufts (flattened icospheres or small planes), and 1–2 rounded rocks (grey cubes with beveled edges).
- **Wood chips:** Tiny scattered cubes around the stump base to suggest recent chopping activity.
- **Optional fence:** A few thin cuboid fence posts with a horizontal rail near the log pile.

## 4. LIGHTING AND PRESENTATION

Briefly suggest a lighting setup to make the flat-shaded low-poly colors pop:

- Use warm directional sunlight filtering through the trees, casting dappled shadows.
- A subtle green-tinted ambient light to suggest a forest clearing environment.
- Soft fill light from the opposite side to prevent harsh shadows on the hut.
- Present from an isometric orthographic camera angle — the hut should look nestled in its woodland setting.
- The log pile and axe should be clearly visible, immediately communicating the building's function.

Make your instructions easy to follow, highly descriptive regarding the 3D shapes (e.g., "extrude," "bevel," "scale down the top face," "icosphere"), and inspiring!
